Is It Legal to Use AI to Call Customers in the US?

Answering calls customers make to you with AI is not telemarketing. Outbound AI calls are different: in February 2024 the FCC ruled that AI-generated voices are "artificial" voices under the TCPA, so marketing calls with an AI voice need the person's prior express written consent.

That is why our US systems lead with inbound answering and consented follow-up: answering every call, texting missed callers back, and following up with people who asked to be contacted. Outbound campaigns, such as maintenance-plan reminders or reactivating an old database, are only built on records with valid consent. Some states add their own telemarketing rules on top of the TCPA, and some require every party's consent before a call is recorded, so the agent can announce recording at the start of each call.

If you are a HIPAA covered entity, such as a dental or medical practice, every vendor in the AI system that handles patient information needs to be able to sign a business associate agreement. We check this during discovery, before anything is built.

Source: FCC declaratory ruling FCC 24-17. General information, not legal advice. Check your own situation with counsel.

What Changes for New York Businesses?

New York is home to many of the law firms, property managers, medical practices and staffing firms that gain most from AI intake and follow-up. It also has two rules worth designing for:

  • The SHIELD Act requires businesses that hold New York residents' private information to keep reasonable security safeguards. Call recordings, transcripts and CRM records created by an AI system count, so we design access, retention and storage with that in mind. (NY Attorney General)
  • NYC Local Law 144 requires a bias audit and notices to candidates before an employer uses an automated employment decision tool in hiring or promotion. If you want AI to screen applicants, for example in healthcare staffing, this is scoped in from the start. (NYC DCWP)

How did we evaluate AI receptionists for med spas?

We compared med spa AI receptionists on six points taken from each vendor's own website: published pricing, booking-system integrations, 24/7 coverage, booking on the call versus sending a link, HIPAA and SOC 2 claims, and the type of med spa each suits. We did not run test calls, so treat this as a shortlist.

  • Published pricing: the vendor's own pricing page, or "not published".
  • Integrations: which med spa booking systems, such as Boulevard, Zenoti or Mindbody, the vendor names.
  • 24/7 coverage: around the clock, or after hours only.
  • Booking: books on the call, texts a link, or takes a message.
  • Compliance claims: what the vendor states about HIPAA, BAAs or SOC 2. We report claims; we did not audit them.
  • Who it fits: business size, existing software and call volume.

Which med spa AI receptionist fits which business?

The right med spa AI receptionist mostly follows your booking software. Zenoti and Boulevard users should try their platform's built-in receptionist first. Spas on Mindbody, Vagaro or Nextech should look at PilotPractice. Boulevard spas wanting people by day can use Abby Connect, and single locations on a budget can start with My AI Front Desk.

Zenoti AI Receptionist

Best for: med spas already running on Zenoti.

Zenoti AI Receptionist answers missed and after-hours calls 24/7, books, reschedules and confirms by voice or text, and writes directly to the Zenoti appointment book with AI attribution. It runs on Zenoti HyperConnect with a Zenoti-provisioned number. Limitation: pricing is not published, it is tied to Zenoti, and the page makes no language or HIPAA claim.

Boulevard (Beau)

Best for: med spas on Boulevard wanting low-cost per-location call cover.

Beau is built into Boulevard and runs always-on, as backup for unanswered calls, or after hours only. It texts callers a booking link or hands off to staff with a summary, and captures treatment interest. Limitation: it books by link rather than on the call, overage is $0.50 a minute, it requires Boulevard, and the page says "Prices may be subject to change".

PilotPractice

Best for: med spas on common aesthetics booking systems wanting an independent med-spa-focused AI front desk.

PilotPractice answers by voice and text 24/7, names Aesthetics Pro, Boulevard, Mindbody, Vagaro and Nextech integrations, claims HIPAA compliance with a BAA and SOC 2 Type II, and can quote treatments and collect deposits. Limitation: there is no published price list; a $499 a month figure appears only in an ROI calculator, not on its pricing page. Languages are not stated.

My AI Front Desk

Best for: single-location med spas wanting an inexpensive general AI phone answerer.

My AI Front Desk handles calls 24/7 with automated scheduling, lists 20+ languages on its pricing page and offers a 7-day free trial with no credit card. Limitation: its med spa page names no booking-system integration or HIPAA claim, and overage is credit-based.

Abby Connect

Best for: med spas on Boulevard wanting AI after hours and live receptionists during the day.

Abby Connect offers an AI receptionist around the clock, live US-based receptionists by day, or both, and books into Boulevard from live availability during the call. AI answering uses one Abby Minute per two minutes. Every plan has a free trial and no setup fee. Limitation: the overage rate is not published, and its homepage does not focus on med spas.

Ruby

Best for: med spas that want a live person on every call.

Ruby answers with live people 24/7, including weekends and holidays, with no AI agent on calls. It states HIPAA-compliant services, offers Spanish and bilingual handling and advertises 5000+ integrations. Limitation: per-minute billing with no stated overage rate, and no med spa booking system is named.

A cheaper AI receptionist is the better choice for most small or low-volume med spas. How should a med spa test an AI receptionist before buying?

A med spa should test an AI receptionist with real scenarios before signing. Check that it books into your own booking system or sends a working link, refuses medical advice and hands clinical questions to staff, collects deposits only through secure links, and stays affordable at your normal monthly call volume.

  1. Count missed, lunchtime and after-hours calls in a normal week.
  2. Book a test consultation into your own booking system during the demo.
  3. Ask about medications, pregnancy and side effects; it should hand those to a clinician.
  4. Check how deposits are taken; never by reading card numbers aloud.
  5. Get any BAA and data terms in writing.
  6. Review transcripts weekly for the first month.

Frequently asked questions

Should an AI receptionist answer clinical questions about treatments, medications or pregnancy?

No. It should answer booking, pricing and logistics questions you approve, and pass clinical questions about medications, pregnancy, side effects or suitability to a qualified clinician, with a summary for the callback.

Which AI receptionist is best for a med spa or wellness practice?

It depends on your booking software. Zenoti and Boulevard users can start with the built-in option; PilotPractice names the widest set of aesthetics integrations in this guide.

Can an AI virtual receptionist increase med spa bookings?

It can recover bookings from calls that currently go unanswered, especially after hours. We do not publish an uplift figure; measure booked consultations before and after, and estimate your gap with the missed-call calculator.

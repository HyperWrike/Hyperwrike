How did we evaluate AI receptionists for law firms?

We compared law firm AI receptionists and legal answering services on six points from each vendor's own website: published pricing, case-management integrations, 24/7 coverage, consultation booking, confidentiality and compliance claims, and the size of firm each suits. Few legal vendors publish compliance certifications, so ask directly. We did not place test calls.

  • Published pricing: the vendor's own pricing page, or "not published".
  • Integrations: which case-management and intake tools, such as Clio or MyCase, the vendor names.
  • 24/7 coverage: around the clock, or after hours only.
  • Booking: books on the call, texts a link, or takes a message.
  • Compliance claims: what the vendor states about HIPAA, BAAs or SOC 2. We report claims; we did not audit them.
  • Who it fits: business size, existing software and call volume.

Which law firm AI receptionist fits which firm?

The right law firm AI receptionist depends on whether you want AI, people or both on first contact. Solo firms on a budget can start with Dialzara. US firms on Clio or MyCase wanting AI and human intake should look at Smith.ai. Firms wanting legal-trained people should compare LEX Reception, Answering Legal and Moneypenny.

Smith.ai

Best for: US firms wanting AI and human legal intake with Clio or MyCase sync.

Smith.ai integrates with Clio, MyCase, Lawmatics and Zapier, runs practice-specific intake and conflict checks, answers 24/7 with bilingual support, and collects payments including LawPay. Limitation: billing is per call, and human plans start at $300 a month for 30 calls.

Ruby

Best for: firms wanting human receptionists on first contact.

Ruby uses live US-based receptionists 24/7/365, books consultations, collects caller details, offers Spanish and bilingual handling, and adds optional AI transcripts and sentiment at no extra cost. Limitation: its legal page names no case-management integrations, and the overage rate is not published.

LEX Reception

Best for: firms wanting legal-trained human intake synced to case management.

LEX Reception uses legal-only human receptionists and states no bots or AI. It integrates with Clio, MyCase, Filevine, Smokeball, Lawmatics, PracticePanther and LawPay, runs 24/7 intake in English and Spanish, and sells plans from 150 to 2,500 minutes a month. Limitation: prices are not readable in its plans page text, and there is no AI option.

Answering Legal

Best for: US firms wanting 24/7 human intake connected to intake or CRM tools.

Answering Legal uses US-based receptionists 24/7/365, connects to Clio Grow, MyCase, Smokeball, PracticePanther, Lawmatics, Litify and Lead Docket, and offers a 400-minute free trial with no long-term contract. Limitation: pricing appears only after a form, and its AI is a website chatbot, not a phone agent.

Moneypenny

Best for: UK firms wanting legal-specialist receptionists with an optional AI layer.

Moneypenny has a UK-based legal receptionist team plus an AI Receptionist that escalates to people, and names Clio, Salesforce, Microsoft Teams and HubSpot integrations. Limitation: pricing is quote-only, minimum terms run 3 to 12 months, and standard hours are 8.30am to 6.00pm, with 24/7 on request.

Dialzara answers 24/7/365 in 10 languages, collects matter details with firm-set questions and books consultations. It connects through Zapier or API. Limitation: no conflict checks, and its law page names no case-management integrations or compliance certifications.

What should a law firm check before automating client intake?

A law firm should check, before automating intake, that the AI or service collects facts without giving legal advice, runs or supports conflict checks, protects confidential information under written terms, syncs to your case-management tool, escalates urgent matters to a lawyer, and tells callers they are not yet clients.

  1. Write intake questions per practice area, and what it must never say.
  2. Confirm conflict checks and case-management sync in a demo.
  3. Get confidentiality, retention and recording terms in writing.
  4. Test urgent matters, such as a court deadline, for escalation.
  5. Tell callers no attorney-client relationship exists yet.
  6. Review transcripts weekly with a supervising lawyer.

Frequently asked questions

Is it ethical for a law firm to use an AI receptionist for client intake?

It can be, if lawyers supervise it, confidentiality is protected, callers are not misled, and it does not give legal advice. Check your own bar or regulator's guidance. This is general information, not legal advice.

Can AI intake cross into the unauthorized practice of law?

It can if it tells callers what their legal rights are or what to do. Keep it to collecting facts, booking consultations and routing urgent matters, and script refusals for legal questions.

What questions should I ask before automating my law firm's client intake?

Ask how it handles conflicts, confidentiality, data retention, call recording, case-management sync, urgent matters, Spanish or other languages, billing at your volume, and who fixes errors. See our law firm AI intake guide.

How do I automate legal client intake without confidentiality risk?

Collect only what intake needs, get written data-processing and retention terms, restrict who reads transcripts, and send records straight into case management rather than email. Basics: what an AI receptionist is.

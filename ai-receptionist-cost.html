What are the three price tiers for AI receptionists?

AI receptionist pricing falls into three tiers: self-serve software you configure yourself, done-for-you setups where a provider configures a standard product for you, and custom AI systems built around your workflow and revenue targets. The price jumps between tiers because the scope changes, not just the call volume.

How much does an AI receptionist cost compared with a human receptionist?

A full-time human receptionist in the US costs a median of about $36,590 a year in wages alone, according to the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, before payroll taxes, benefits, holidays and sick cover. That buys about 40 hours of coverage in a 168-hour week. An AI receptionist covers all 168 hours.

The fairer comparison is not AI instead of people, but what each does best. AI handles volume, after-hours calls, instant callbacks and repetitive booking. People handle complaints, sensitive conversations and complex sales. See our side-by-side: AI receptionist vs answering service vs in-house staff.

Which factors change the price of an AI receptionist?

  • Call volume and minutes. Most software prices by minutes or calls, with overage charges above the plan.
  • Integrations. Booking into Dentrix, ServiceTitan, Jane, a CRM or a practice-management system costs more than sending an email summary.
  • Channels. Voice only is cheapest. Adding SMS, WhatsApp and web chat adds cost and usually adds more booked appointments.
  • Outbound work. Reminder calls, speed-to-lead callbacks and database reactivation are separate jobs from answering inbound calls.
  • Languages and compliance. Multilingual agents and HIPAA, UK GDPR or UAE PDPL requirements add setup work.
  • Who maintains it. Self-serve means your team updates scripts. A retainer means the provider monitors and improves it.

How do you work out whether an AI receptionist pays for itself?

Multiply the calls you miss each month by the share that would have booked, then by the value of a booking. If that number is larger than the monthly cost, the system pays for itself.

  1. Count missed calls for one normal week from your phone system, then multiply by 4.3.
  2. Estimate how many of those callers would have booked (your normal booking rate is a fair start).
  3. Multiply bookings by your average first-visit or first-job value.
  4. Add no-show losses and leads that never got a callback.
  5. Compare the monthly total with the price tiers above.

Example: 60 missed calls a month × 30% booking rate × $400 average first job = $7,200 a month of revenue going to competitors, before counting no-shows.

Is an AI receptionist cheaper than a human receptionist?

Usually, yes. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the median receptionist wage at about $36,590 a year before taxes and benefits, and one person covers roughly 40 of the 168 hours in a week. An AI receptionist answers every hour of the week for a fraction of that. A person is still better at sensitive or complex calls, so many businesses use AI for volume and after-hours, with staff for escalations.

What hidden costs should I check before buying an AI receptionist?

Check setup fees, per-minute or per-call overage charges, extra charges for phone numbers and SMS, integration fees for your booking or CRM software, contract length, and who pays for changes to scripts. Ask for the total monthly cost at your real call volume, not the entry price.

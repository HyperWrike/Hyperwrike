Why do signed solar customers cancel before installation?

Signed solar customers cancel before installation because the sale is not finished until the system is on the roof. In the weeks between signing and install, buyer's remorse, financing worries, permit delays, silence from the installer and competitors' re-pitches all build doubt, while the sales rep has moved on to new deals.

Most of these causes come down to silence and unanswered questions. Customers who know what happens next, and when, have fewer reasons to change their mind. The same idea drives our roofing AI work, where estimates also need steady follow-up.

How does AI keep signed solar customers engaged?

AI keeps signed customers engaged by sending clear updates at every milestone, answering routine questions instantly by text, email or phone, and checking in between milestones. When a customer sounds doubtful or mentions cancelling, the system alerts a named person on your team straight away, with the full conversation history.

  1. Welcome message after signing: what happens next and who to contact.
  2. Milestone updates: site survey, design, permit, utility approval and install date.
  3. Instant answers to routine questions about timelines and process, using information you approve.
  4. Regular check-ins between milestones so customers never go weeks without hearing from you.
  5. Risk flags: doubtful replies, missed calls or competitor mentions alert a human closer at once.
  6. After installation: review request and referral sequence.

Which signs show a solar customer is about to cancel?

Common warning signs are questions about the cancellation process, sudden worry about financing or monthly payments, mention of another quote, unanswered messages, missed site surveys and frustration about delays. The AI looks for these in replies and call notes, and hands each flagged customer to a person rather than trying to argue them out of it.

Does the FTC cooling-off rule affect solar cancellations?

Yes, in many US cases. The FTC Cooling-Off Rule gives buyers until midnight of the third business day to cancel covered sales made at their home, for purchases of $25 or more. State laws may add further rights. Any AI follow-up must respect cancellation rights and never discourage a valid cancellation.

Source: FTC: Buyer's remorse and the Cooling-Off Rule. Outbound AI calls and texts also fall under consent rules; see FCC declaratory ruling on AI-generated voices (FCC 24-17). General information, not legal advice. Check with your own counsel or compliance adviser.
